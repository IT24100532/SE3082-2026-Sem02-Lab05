Exercise 5: Fibonacci with task parallelism
Run on: MacBook Air (Apple Silicon, 8 cores), Apple clang + libomp. Build with: make
Program: exercise05.c (run: ./exercise05 40)

How it is parallelised:
- main starts a parallel region, and "single" lets one thread make the first call fib(n).
- Inside fib, the two recursive calls become two tasks (#pragma omp task). Any free
  thread in the team can pick up a task.
- i and j are shared so the tasks can write their results back to the parent.
- #pragma omp taskwait makes the parent wait for both tasks before returning i + j.
- A cutoff (n < 20) switches to the plain serial function. Creating a task for tiny
  calls costs more time than the call itself, so without the cutoff the parallel
  version is slower than the serial one.

Output for fib(40) = 102334155 (the same answer in every run):
serial:               0.341 s
parallel, 1 thread:   0.326 s
parallel, 2 threads:  0.191 s
parallel, 4 threads:  0.087 s   (about 3.9x faster than serial)
parallel, 8 threads:  0.169 s

8 threads was slower than 4 here. This machine has 4 fast cores and 4 slower
efficiency cores, so the extra 4 threads add little and bring more overhead.
